# A7 Module 2: A/C Diagnosis and Service

This module covers how technicians diagnose and service automotive A/C systems. You will learn to use pressure gauges, detect leaks, and properly recover and recharge refrigerant. A/C service requires careful procedures because refrigerant is regulated by law and must be handled responsibly. Mastering these skills will make you an essential team member at any repair shop.

---

## A/C Pressure Testing (High and Low Side)

An A/C system has two sides: the **high side** (between the compressor outlet and the expansion device) and the **low side** (between the expansion device and the compressor inlet). Measuring pressures on both sides tells you a lot about system health. Normal low-side pressure is typically 25 to 45 PSI, and normal high-side pressure is roughly 150 to 300 PSI, depending on ambient temperature and the specific vehicle. Pressures that are both too low may indicate a low charge. Pressures that are both too high may point to a condenser problem or overcharge.

---

## Manifold Gauge Set Reading

A **manifold gauge set** is the primary tool for A/C diagnosis. It has two gauges connected by hoses: a blue gauge and hose for the low side and a red gauge and hose for the high side. A yellow center hose connects to a refrigerant source or recovery machine. You connect the blue hose to the low-side service port and the red hose to the high-side service port. With the engine running and A/C on maximum, you read both gauges and compare the pressures to specifications. Learning to interpret these readings is one of the most important A/C diagnostic skills.

---

## Refrigerant Leak Detection

Refrigerant leaks reduce cooling performance and are harmful to the environment. Technicians use several methods to find leaks. An **electronic leak detector** sniffs for refrigerant gas and beeps when it finds a concentration. **UV dye** can be added to the system; it glows under an ultraviolet light at the leak point. **Soap bubble solution** can be applied to fittings and hoses to visually spot bubbling at a leak. Common leak locations include hose connections, the compressor shaft seal, the condenser, and the evaporator. Finding and fixing leaks before recharging is essential.

---

## A/C Performance Testing

A basic **performance test** tells you whether the A/C is cooling properly. With the engine running, A/C set to maximum cooling, and the blower on high, you insert a thermometer into the center dash vent. The outlet air temperature should typically be 35 to 50 degrees Fahrenheit, depending on ambient temperature and humidity. You also check that the compressor clutch is engaging, the condenser fan is running, and there are no unusual noises. Comparing the vent temperature and system pressures to specifications helps you determine if the system is working correctly or needs further diagnosis.

---

## Compressor Clutch Diagnosis

Many compressors use an electromagnetic **clutch** to engage and disengage the compressor from the engine's drive belt. When the A/C is turned on, the clutch should click and the front plate of the compressor should spin with the belt. If the clutch does not engage, the problem could be a blown fuse, a faulty relay, a bad clutch coil, low refrigerant triggering a low-pressure cutoff switch, or a wiring issue. You can test the clutch coil by measuring its resistance with a multimeter and by applying direct battery voltage to see if it engages. Always check the simple things first.

---

## Refrigerant Recovery and Recycling

Federal law under the Clean Air Act requires that refrigerant be properly **recovered** before opening an A/C system for repair. You cannot simply vent refrigerant into the atmosphere. A certified **recovery/recycling machine** connects to the vehicle's service ports and pumps the refrigerant out of the system into a storage tank. The machine filters and cleans the refrigerant so it can be reused. Technicians must be EPA Section 609 certified to purchase and handle automotive refrigerant. Proper recovery protects the environment and ensures you comply with the law.

---

## System Evacuation and Recharge

After a repair, the A/C system must be **evacuated** and **recharged** before it can work again. Evacuation uses a vacuum pump connected through the manifold gauge set to pull all air and moisture out of the system. The system is held under vacuum (typically 29 inches of mercury) for at least 30 minutes. If the vacuum holds steady, there are no leaks. Then the system is **recharged** by adding the exact amount of refrigerant specified by the manufacturer, measured by weight. Overcharging or undercharging will cause poor performance and can damage components.

---

## A/C System Oil Requirements

The A/C compressor needs **refrigerant oil** to lubricate its internal moving parts. This oil circulates through the entire system mixed with the refrigerant. Different systems require different oil types: **PAG oil** (polyalkylene glycol) is most common with R-134a systems, while some R-1234yf systems may require specific PAG formulations. **POE oil** (polyol ester) is sometimes used in hybrid vehicles with electric compressors. Using the wrong oil type can cause compressor failure. When replacing components, you must add back the correct amount and type of oil to replace what was lost from the removed part.
