# G1 Module 1: Engine and Drivetrain Maintenance

This module covers the routine maintenance procedures that keep engines and drivetrains healthy and reliable. These are the services you will perform most often as an entry-level technician. Oil changes, coolant flushes, belt inspections, and fluid services may seem simple, but doing them correctly prevents costly breakdowns and extends the life of a vehicle. You will learn what each service involves, why it matters, and what to look for during each procedure.

---

## Oil and Filter Change Procedures

An oil change is the most common maintenance service in any shop. Engine oil lubricates moving parts, reduces friction, and carries away heat and contaminants. Over time, oil breaks down and the filter becomes saturated with debris. To change the oil, you raise the vehicle safely on a lift, place a drain pan underneath, remove the drain plug, and let the old oil flow out. Then you replace the drain plug, remove the old oil filter, install a new pre-lubricated filter, and add the correct type and amount of fresh oil. Always check for leaks afterward and verify the oil level on the dipstick.

---

## Coolant Flush and Fill

The cooling system prevents the engine from overheating by circulating coolant through the engine block and radiator. Over time, coolant loses its protective properties and can become acidic, corroding internal components. A **coolant flush** removes all the old coolant and any contaminants from the system. The process involves draining the old coolant, flushing the system with clean water or a flushing solution, then refilling with the correct type and mixture of coolant, typically a 50/50 mix of antifreeze and distilled water. After filling, you must bleed any trapped air from the system to prevent hot spots and overheating.

## Serpentine Belt Inspection

The **serpentine belt** is a single, long belt that drives multiple accessories including the alternator, power steering pump, water pump, and air conditioning compressor. During an inspection, you look for cracks, fraying, glazing, or chunks of missing rubber. Modern belts made of EPDM rubber may look fine on the surface but wear thin over time, so a belt wear gauge is used to measure rib depth. You also check belt tension and the condition of the belt tensioner, which is a spring-loaded pulley that keeps the belt tight. A squealing noise on startup often points to a worn belt or weak tensioner.

## Spark Plug Replacement

Spark plugs ignite the air-fuel mixture inside each cylinder. Over time, the electrodes wear down and deposits build up, making the spark weaker and causing misfires or poor fuel economy. To replace them, you remove the ignition coil or spark plug wire, use a spark plug socket with a rubber insert to carefully unscrew the old plug, and inspect it for signs of engine problems like oil fouling or overheating. You then gap the new plug to the manufacturer's specification using a gap tool, thread it in by hand to avoid cross-threading, and torque it to spec. Always replace plugs at the interval recommended by the vehicle manufacturer.

---

## Air Filter Replacement

The engine air filter prevents dust, pollen, and debris from entering the engine. A clogged filter restricts airflow, which reduces performance and fuel economy. Replacing it is one of the simplest maintenance tasks. You locate the airbox, which is usually a large plastic housing near the front of the engine compartment, unclip or unscrew the lid, remove the old filter, and inspect the housing for debris. Install the new filter making sure it seats properly, then close the housing securely. Most manufacturers recommend replacement every 15,000 to 30,000 miles, but driving on dusty roads may require more frequent changes.

## Transmission Fluid Check and Service

Transmission fluid lubricates gears, cools the transmission, and provides hydraulic pressure for shifting in automatic transmissions. To check the fluid on vehicles with a dipstick, the engine is typically running and warmed up. You pull the dipstick, wipe it clean, reinsert it, and check the level and condition. Healthy fluid is usually red or pink and translucent. Dark, brown, or burnt-smelling fluid indicates it needs service. A transmission fluid service can range from a simple drain-and-fill to a complete flush using a machine that exchanges all the fluid. Always use the exact fluid type specified by the manufacturer, as using the wrong type can cause shifting problems or damage.

---

## Differential Fluid Service

The **differential** is a gear assembly that allows the drive wheels to spin at different speeds when turning. It contains thick gear oil that lubricates the ring and pinion gears and bearings inside. Over time, this fluid breaks down from heat and metal wear particles accumulate. To service it, you remove the fill plug first to ensure you can add new fluid, then remove the drain plug or the differential cover to drain the old fluid. After cleaning the mating surfaces and reinstalling the cover with a new gasket or sealant, you fill with the correct weight and type of gear oil until it reaches the fill hole. This service is often overlooked but is important for long differential life.

---

## Timing Belt/Chain Inspection

The **timing belt** or **timing chain** synchronizes the rotation of the crankshaft and camshaft so that valves open and close at exactly the right time. A timing belt is made of reinforced rubber and must be replaced at the manufacturer's recommended interval, typically every 60,000 to 100,000 miles. If a timing belt breaks on an interference engine, the pistons can strike the valves and cause severe engine damage. A timing chain is metal and generally lasts much longer, but it can stretch over time, causing rattling noises and timing issues. During inspection, technicians listen for unusual noises and check for proper timing alignment marks.
