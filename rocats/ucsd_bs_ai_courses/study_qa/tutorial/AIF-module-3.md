# Module 3 -- Probabilistic AI

## Overview

The real world is full of **uncertainty**: sensors are noisy, information is incomplete, and outcomes are unpredictable. **Probabilistic AI** uses the mathematics of probability to represent and reason about uncertainty, enabling AI systems to make rational decisions even when they do not have complete information.

## Probability Basics

A **probability** is a number between 0 and 1 representing the likelihood of an event.

- **P(A)**: the probability that event A occurs.
- **Joint probability**: P(A, B) is the probability that both A and B occur.
- **Conditional probability**: P(A | B) = P(A, B) / P(B) is the probability of A given that B is known to be true.
- **Marginalization**: P(A) = sum over all b of P(A, B = b). You can compute the probability of A by summing over all possibilities for B.

## Bayes' Theorem

**Bayes' theorem** relates conditional probabilities:

```
P(H | E) = P(E | H) * P(H) / P(E)
```

- **P(H)**: the **prior** -- your belief about hypothesis H before seeing evidence.
- **P(E | H)**: the **likelihood** -- how probable the evidence is if H is true.
- **P(H | E)**: the **posterior** -- your updated belief after seeing evidence E.

Bayes' theorem is the foundation of medical diagnosis systems, spam filters, and any AI that updates its beliefs with new data.

## Bayesian Networks

A **Bayesian network** (also called a belief network) is a directed acyclic graph where:

- Each **node** represents a random variable.
- Each **edge** represents a direct probabilistic dependency.
- Each node has a **conditional probability table (CPT)** specifying P(node | parents).

Bayesian networks compactly represent joint probability distributions and allow efficient inference.

### Inference in Bayesian Networks

- **Exact inference**: algorithms like **variable elimination** compute exact probabilities by systematically summing out variables.
- **Approximate inference**: methods like **sampling** (Monte Carlo) estimate probabilities when exact computation is too expensive.

## Hidden Markov Models (HMMs)

An **HMM** models a sequence of observations generated by a process with hidden (unobservable) states.

- **Transition probabilities**: the chance of moving from one hidden state to another.
- **Emission probabilities**: the chance of observing a particular output given the current hidden state.

Key algorithms:

- **Forward algorithm**: computes the probability of an observation sequence.
- **Viterbi algorithm**: finds the most likely sequence of hidden states.

HMMs are used in speech recognition, gene sequence analysis, and part-of-speech tagging.

## Decision Making Under Uncertainty

- **Expected utility**: a rational agent chooses the action that maximizes the expected utility (average reward weighted by probability).
- **Markov Decision Processes (MDPs)**: extend decision making to sequential settings, forming the foundation of reinforcement learning.

## Key Takeaways

- Probability provides a principled framework for reasoning under uncertainty.
- Bayes' theorem lets AI systems update beliefs as new evidence arrives.
- Bayesian networks compactly encode complex probabilistic relationships.
- HMMs model sequential data with hidden states and are foundational for temporal reasoning.
