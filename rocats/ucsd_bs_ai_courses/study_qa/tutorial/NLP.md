# NLP & Applications

This section covers the natural language processing and applications courses in the UCSD BS AI program: **CSE 153/153R** (Natural Language Processing) and **CSE 158/158R** (Recommender Systems and Web Mining). These courses teach machines to understand, generate, and reason about human language, and to build systems that predict user preferences from behavioral data.

## What You Will Learn

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)** is the branch of AI focused on the interaction between computers and human language. You will learn how text is represented numerically, how sequence models capture the structure of language, and how modern transformer architectures power systems like chatbots, translators, and search engines. You will also learn how recommender systems leverage user behavior to personalize content.

## Module Breakdown

- **[Module 1 -- Text Processing & Representations](NLP-module-1.md)**
  Learn how raw text is cleaned, tokenized, and converted into numerical representations that machines can work with.

- **[Module 2 -- Sequence Models & Transformers](NLP-module-2.md)**
  Explore the architectures that model sequential data, from RNNs to the attention-based transformers that dominate modern NLP.

- **[Module 3 -- Recommender Systems](NLP-module-3.md)**
  Understand how collaborative filtering, content-based methods, and hybrid systems predict what users will enjoy.

## Why It Matters for AI

- **Search engines** rank documents based on language understanding.
- **Virtual assistants** (Siri, Alexa) parse and respond to natural language queries.
- **Machine translation** breaks down language barriers in real time.
- **Recommender systems** drive engagement on platforms like Netflix, Amazon, and Spotify.

NLP is one of the fastest-growing areas of AI, with large language models reshaping how humans interact with technology.

## Tips for Success

- Work with real text data (reviews, news articles, social media posts).
- Experiment with pre-trained models from Hugging Face.
- Pay attention to data preprocessing -- it often determines the success of an NLP project.
